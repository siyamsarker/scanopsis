# Code of conduct

This page describes how we treat each other in the Scanopsis community, and what to do when something goes wrong.

**Audience:** Everyone · **Back to:** [README](README.md) · [All guides](README.md#documentation)

---

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Scanopsis to be a harassment-free experience for
everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability,
ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of
experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal
appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation. We act in ways that keep the community open, welcoming, diverse,
inclusive and healthy.

## What we expect

Good behaviour looks like this:

- Showing empathy and kindness toward other people.
- Respecting different opinions, viewpoints and experiences.
- Giving constructive feedback, and accepting it gracefully.
- Owning our mistakes, apologising to those affected, and learning from them.
- Focusing on what is best for the whole community, not just for ourselves.

We do not accept:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any
  kind.
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political
  attacks.
- Public or private harassment.
- Publishing other people's private information, such as a physical or e-mail
  address, without their explicit permission.
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be seen as inappropriate in a
  professional setting.

## Where it applies

This code applies in all community spaces: issues, pull requests,
discussions, and any other place where you represent the project, for example
when you post from an official account or speak for the project at an event.

## Report a problem

Report unacceptable behaviour to the maintainer at
[siyam.ts@gmail.com](mailto:siyam.ts@gmail.com). Every report is reviewed
promptly and fairly, and the privacy and safety of the person who reports is
respected.

The maintainer may remove, edit or reject comments, commits, code, issues and
other contributions that break this code, and explains the reason when that
is appropriate.

## What happens after a report

The maintainer decides on the consequence based on its impact on the
community:

| Step | When | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Correction | Inappropriate language or other unprofessional or unwelcome behaviour. | A private, written warning that explains what was wrong. A public apology may be asked for. |
| 2. Warning | A violation through one incident or a series of actions. | A warning with consequences if it goes on, and no contact with the people involved for a set time, in community spaces and outside them. Breaking these terms may lead to a ban. |
| 3. Temporary ban | A serious violation, including sustained inappropriate behaviour. | A ban from any interaction or public communication with the community for a set time. Breaking these terms may lead to a permanent ban. |
| 4. Permanent ban | A pattern of violations, harassment of a person, or aggression toward or disparagement of groups of people. | A permanent ban from any public interaction within the community. |
